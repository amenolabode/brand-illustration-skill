# System specification and validation

## Required specification

### Form

- Base grid and key angles.
- Silhouette complexity at icon, standard and display sizes.
- Stroke-to-canvas ratios.
- Corner radii, terminals and joining logic.
- Projection and depth direction.

### Surface

- Pattern construction from primitives.
- Micro, standard and display density tiers.
- Clipping, overlap and registration rules.
- Pattern-free reduced state.
- Minimum line width and gap for screen and print.

### Colour

Assign roles:

- Field/background.
- Front face or primary subject.
- Structure/outline.
- Depth/shadow.
- Signal/accent.
- Quiet texture.

Define one-colour, grayscale, dark-field and high-contrast variants.

### Composition

- Object-to-canvas scale.
- Safe area and crop tolerance.
- Number of focal objects.
- Background activity.
- Relationship between utility icons, editorial objects and full scenes.

### Motion

- Two to four verbs native to the source.
- What remains fixed while one explanatory component moves.
- Duration, easing, loop and phase relationships.
- Reduced-motion frame.

## Proof matrix

Test at minimum:

- 16–24 px utility icon.
- 48–96 px product pictogram.
- 256–512 px editorial object.
- Wide technical or narrative scene.
- Static and motion states.
- Screen and print simulation.

Include simple, emotional and abstract subjects. A system that only handles one content type is not yet proven.

## Evaluation questions

- Meaning: does the source reinforce the promise rather than only the category?
- Recognition: are subjects clear before surface detail is examined?
- Ownership: is the system identifiable without the logo or primary colour?
- Range: can it explain products, emotions, processes and abstract ideas?
- Reduction: does the silhouette survive when detail disappears?
- Motion: do transitions follow the source’s behavior?
- Production: can the team reproduce it reliably?
- Accessibility: does meaning survive colour and motion constraints?
- Stewardship: are provenance and permissions documented?

## Failure diagnoses

### Looks generic without the pattern

The pattern is compensating for weak structural rules. Extend the source into silhouette, depth, composition or motion.

### Looks beautiful but cannot explain products

Simplify subject symbols and reserve the brand grammar for the secondary layer.

### Falls apart at small sizes

Create a reduced state. Replace patterns with one structural band, cut or junction that preserves the source logic.

### Every asset has the same composition

Consistency has been confused with repetition. Preserve invariants but vary scene type, scale, crop and hierarchy.

### Cultural source feels superficial

Stop production. Revisit provenance, meaning and practitioner involvement or select a different source.

## Acceptance test

A new designer should be able to receive only the brief and specification, then create an unshown asset that:

1. Is recognizable.
2. Uses the documented primitives and operations.
3. Preserves invariants.
4. Passes the minimum-size state.
5. Is judged part of the family without relying solely on colour.

Review the artifact, not whether the designer repeated document wording.
